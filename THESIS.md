## 1. Thesis (one paragraph, canonical)

Two measurable input curves — AI capability/cost and robotics hardware deployment/cost — are converging on labor-cost thresholds across cohorts of work. When you triangulate four downstream metrics (cost-per-task crossover, robot density, labor-share-of-GDP, embodied-AI capex), the curves intersect human labor between roughly 2028 and 2035 — earliest in industrial/warehouse, latest in personal-dexterous-service. But the substitution curve is not dictated by capability or cost alone. It is clipped by five execution-layer constraints — grid capacity, robot manufacturing capacity, reliability thresholds, demographic counter-pressure, and regulatory/liability friction — that bend the exponential into a constrained S-curve with a knowable shape. The macro layer reinforces the trajectory: capital allocation toward labor automation is public, visible, and incentive-driven (not conspiratorial), and the transition is being financed via fiscal expansion that debases savings. The combined outcome is K-shaped: cheap goods, scarce assets, abundant money. The defensive posture is positional — own scarce productive assets (productive equity, real estate, Bitcoin among them), and develop skills on the not-yet-automatable side of the cohort map.

## 3. Article outline

### Part 1 — The Trajectory (the data showpiece)

**1.1 What "marriage at scale" actually means — four metrics, not one**

Triangulation framing. Each metric is independently sourced. If all four converge on the 2028-2035 window from independent data, the thesis is robust. If they diverge, we publish the divergence honestly.

- M1: Cost-per-task crossover ($/hr robot fully-loaded vs $/hr human by task class)
- M2: Robot density (operational embodied-AI units / 10K workers, by sector)
- M3: Labor-share-of-GDP (wages as % of GDP — BLS/BEA + OECD)
- M4: Annual embodied-AI capex (USD flowing into AI-robotics infrastructure)

**1.2 The two measured input curves**

- AI capability/cost (Leg 1): Epoch AI compute trends, METR task-length doubling, inference $/Mtok trajectory, benchmark saturation across ARC-AGI, GPQA, SWE-Bench, MMLU
- Robotics deployment/cost (Leg 2): IFR World Robotics annual installations + stock, humanoid unit-cost curve (Unitree retail → Figure/Optimus targets), VLA capability via OpenVLA, RT-2/RT-X, π0
- **Honest disclosure inline:** Legs 1 and 2 are measured. The intersection is derived, not directly observed. Article states this explicitly.

**1.3 The execution layer (5 constraints — the part that gives the article credibility against pundit doom-curves)**

- C1: Grid / energy — EIA, IEA forecasts, FERC interconnection queue, hyperscaler PPA velocity
- C2: Manufacturing capacity — humanoid gigafactory lead times, China vs US/EU output trajectory
- C3: Reliability threshold — the autonomous-vehicle 15-year lesson, embodied-AI MTBF where published
- C4: Demographic counter-pressure — UN World Population Prospects labor-force contractions in JP/KR/IT/DE/CN
- C5: Regulation / liability / insurance — OSHA, EU AI Act, US state-level patchwork

Each constraint is treated as an S-curve clipper, not a hard stop.

**1.4 Cohort map — who gets affected when**

- 2026-2029: Warehouse pick-pack, predictable logistics, structured manufacturing
- 2029-2032: Commercial cleaning, food-service prep, basic construction support
- 2032-2035: Skilled trades on legacy infrastructure, personal care, dexterous service
- Persistent past 2035: Creative judgment, complex interpersonal, novel-environment dexterity

Cohort sizing from BLS occupational employment data (US) + China NBS labor data.

**1.5 The 2028-2035 window with falsifiable checkpoints**

The article commits to specific predictions readers can verify in 12 / 24 / 48 months:
- 2026 checkpoints: Mag7 capex run-rate, humanoid Series funding totals, IFR YoY install growth, grid interconnection queue length
- 2028 checkpoints: First sector with >10K commercial humanoids in routine operation; first documented cost-per-task crossover
- 2030 checkpoints: Robot density inflection in a target sector; first visible labor-share-of-GDP turn
- 2032-2035: Convergence window — multi-metric confirmation or revision

### Part 2 — The Macro Forces (capital allocation + financing)

**2.1 Who's driving it — structural, not conspiratorial**

Capital allocators are public and named:
- Mag7 capex hockey-stick 2023→2025 (10-K filings)
- Stargate $500B announcement (OpenAI / SoftBank / Oracle / MGX)
- Saudi PIF AI fund, G42 (UAE), MIIT humanoid roadmap (China)
- Andreessen "techno-optimist manifesto," Karp Foundry posture, Schmidt SCSP report

Frame: this is incentive alignment, not conspiracy. Labor is structurally expensive and demographically contracting. Capital is cheap. The buildout follows.

**2.2 How the transition is being financed**

- US fiscal trajectory + CBO deficit projections
- Fed balance sheet, M2 growth, real rates
- Mag7 capex-to-revenue ratios
- Sovereign-fund AUM deployed to AI/automation

Argument: monetary debasement is one financing channel for the transition. Savers in fiat are harvested. Owners of scarce assets are not.

### Part 3 — K-Shape and Positioning

**2.3 The K-shaped outcome**

- Cheap goods (deflation in tradeables — AI/robotics expand supply)
- Scarce assets (inflation in non-reproducible — productive equity, real estate in supply-constrained markets, monetary scarcity assets)
- Abundant money (continued fiscal/monetary expansion)
- Historical analog: 2009-2024 already showed early K-shape from software automation alone

**2.4 Positioning — analyst voice, multiple options**

Two axes of positioning:

*Asset side:*
- Productive equity (firms doing the automating — broad indices vs concentrated bets)
- Real estate in supply-constrained markets
- Bitcoin and monetary-scarcity assets — framed as one scarcity exposure among several
- Counterarguments acknowledged: BTC volatility, regulatory risk, custody risk, opportunity cost vs equity

*Skill side:*
- Where you sit on the cohort map matters more than how much you save
- Practical guidance: which skill clusters remain on the not-yet-automatable side longest

## 4. Methodology commitments

These are the non-negotiable rules. They differentiate the article from a pundit Substack.

1. **Public data appendix.** Every chart in the article links to the raw CSV in the article repo. Reader can rebuild any chart.
2. **`sources.md` tracking.** Every claim cited. Schema: id, source, URL, retrieval date, specific series/data point, status (proposed/pulled/verified/cited), confidence, caveats.
3. **`methodology.md`.** How we normalize across series with different units/baselines. How we project (constraint-clipped S-curves, NOT naive exponential extrapolation). How we handle Leg-3-as-derived honestly.
4. **Version control on predictions.** Article ends with explicit revisit date. Optional 12-month follow-up post scoring how the checkpoints fared.
5. **No motivated reasoning.** When data contradicts the thesis, publish the contradiction. Pruning a leg is acceptable; cherry-picking is not.

---

## 5. Open thesis risks (honest list)

1. **Projection methodology risk.** Naive trend-line extrapolation of exponentials is the universal pundit failure mode. Mitigation: constraint-aware S-curves with explicit clip mechanisms. Show the unconstrained-vs-constrained gap visually.
2. **Falsifiability risk.** Vague predictions age into "obvious in hindsight" or "wrong but unmemorable." Mitigation: §1.5 checkpoints with specific thresholds + dates.
3. **Section-2-to-BTC handoff risk.** Reads as motivated reasoning if rushed. Mitigation: K-shape argument is the load-bearing piece; BTC is one element of scarcity exposure, not the conclusion. Counterarguments inline.
4. **Tone risk.** "Robots take jobs, buy bitcoin" is a tired genre. Mitigation: demographics framing (robots fill jobs we can't staff) + Jevons (induced demand) + skill-side guidance (where to position) elevate the analysis.
5. **Leg 3 weak-data risk.** "The marriage itself" is mostly forecasts, not measured deployment. Mitigation: leg renamed and reframed as *execution layer* — observable constraints (grid, manufacturing, reliability, demographics, regulation) clipping the curve, NOT pretending we have deployment data we don't have.
