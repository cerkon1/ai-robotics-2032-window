# The 2032 Window — Thesis

*The 2032 Window: When AI and Robotics Cross the Cost-of-Labor Line*
Three-part Substack series. Analyst voice. Counterarguments inline.

---

## The thesis, in one paragraph

Two measurable input curves — AI capability/cost and robotics hardware deployment/cost — are converging on labor-cost thresholds across cohorts of work. When you triangulate four downstream metrics (cost-per-task crossover, robot density, labor-share-of-GDP, embodied-AI capex), the curves intersect human labor between roughly 2028 and 2035 — earliest in industrial/warehouse, latest in personal-dexterous-service. But the substitution curve is not dictated by capability or cost alone. It is clipped by five execution-layer constraints — grid capacity, robot manufacturing capacity, reliability thresholds, demographic counter-pressure, and regulatory/liability friction — that bend the exponential into a constrained S-curve with a knowable shape. The macro layer reinforces the trajectory: capital allocation toward labor automation is public, visible, and incentive-driven (not conspiratorial), and the transition is being financed via fiscal expansion that debases savings. The combined outcome is K-shaped: cheap goods, scarce assets, abundant money. The defensive posture is positional — own scarce productive assets (productive equity, real estate, Bitcoin among them), and develop skills on the not-yet-automatable side of the cohort map.

---

## The four-metric triangulation

The thesis does not rest on a single proxy. It rests on four metrics that, when they point at the same window from independent data, make the convergence robust:

1. **Cost-per-task crossover** — fully-loaded robot operating cost ($/hr) against the wage of the cohort of workers it can substitute for
2. **Robot density** — operational embodied-AI units per ten thousand workers in a sector
3. **Labor share of GDP** — wages as a percentage of national output; the macro signal that the K-shape is showing up in aggregate
4. **Annual AI/robotics capex** — capital expenditure flowing into the buildout

When all four point at the same window from independent data sources, the thesis is robust. When they diverge, the article publishes the divergence. The series does not pre-commit to convergence; the data does.

## The three parts

**Part 1 — The Trajectory.** The two input curves (METR autonomy + Epoch AI compute; IFR robotics + humanoid pricing). The cohort map. The five execution-layer constraints (grid, manufacturing, reliability, demographics, regulation) that bend exponentials into a constrained S-curve. The 2028-2035 window, with specific falsifiable checkpoints.

**Part 2 — The Macro Forces.** Who is paying for the buildout (Mag7 capex hockey-stick, Stargate, sovereign-fund allocations, named capital allocators). How the transition is being financed (US fiscal trajectory, monetary aggregates, capex-to-revenue ratios). Why the actors are visible and incentive-aligned rather than conspiratorial.

**Part 3 — K-Shape and Positioning.** The K-shape outcome on the household balance sheet (CPI components, labor share of GDP, scarce-asset performance). The skill side — which cohorts retain durable income past 2035. The asset side — productive equity, real estate in supply-constrained markets, monetary-scarcity assets (Bitcoin treated as one element of a scarce-asset allocation, not the answer).
